Overview

The project migrated a display platform from a Linux single-board computer to a microcontroller-based architecture.

My work

  • Developed hardware and embedded software for the display interface.
  • Supported the transition to an ESP32-P4 architecture.
  • Designed for a custom MIPI-DSI system.

Results

  • Reduced boot time from approximately 30 seconds to under 2 seconds.
  • Reduced current draw from 0.8 A to 14 mA.
  • Cut bill-of-materials cost by 88%.

Impact

The new architecture simplified assembly while making the platform faster, more efficient, and less expensive.

Project Details

During Summer 2026, I had the opportunity to intern at Rheyo, an early-stage startup developing dental products that translate rigorous chemistry, formulation, and device engineering into simple clinical workflows. My project focused on reducing the technical and manufacturing risks of the company’s proprietary electrically mediated delivery platform for teeth whitening by lowering manufacturing cost, boot time, and power consumption while simplifying assembly. This required both hardware and software development. I explored replacing the Raspberry Pi and HDMI display with an ESP32-P4 microcontroller and a MIPI DSI display to create a less expensive and more efficient device.

Throughout the project, I made one change at a time so that I could isolate the causes of any problems. I first replaced the Raspberry Pi with a Toradex AM62 compute module, which supported a MIPI DSI display and allowed me to continue using Python. After successfully running the existing software on the AM62 module with Toradex’s display, I designed a custom printed circuit board (PCB) to connect the module to a less expensive MIPI DSI display. I then replaced the AM62 module with an ESP32-P4 development kit and rewrote the Python code in C. To validate the hardware connections and firmware, I designed another custom PCB to connect the display to the ESP32-P4. Once these were tested, I designed a final PCB that integrated all the required components and fit inside the device’s enclosure. By the end of the internship, I had tested the board and verified that the system functioned correctly.

The project reduced the bill of materials (BOM) cost by 88% and shortened boot time from 30 seconds to under 2 seconds. It also reduced current draw by approximately 98%, from 0.8 A to 14 mA, extending estimated battery life by about 57 times. Integrating the components onto a single board also simplified assembly.

This internship gave me the opportunity to take ownership of a project and lead it from initial exploration through design, implementation, and testing. I strengthened my PCB design skills by learning to meet MIPI DSI requirements, including controlled impedance and trace length matching. Making changes incrementally also taught me the value of a systematic approach to debugging and validating a design. Beyond the technical work, I gained insight into how an early-stage startup operates, develops and markets products, and balances engineering decisions with business needs.
